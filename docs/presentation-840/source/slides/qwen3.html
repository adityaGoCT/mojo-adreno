Mojo on the Adreno 840 · 9 · qwen3/ · prism-ml/Ternary-Bonsai-8B-mlx-2bit · decode: SM8850 reference phone · prompt: Galaxy S26 Ultra

The third model is the largest: PrismML’s Ternary-Bonsai-8B, which is Qwen3-8B with every projection and the output head ternary, 2.19 gigabytes on the GPU. The phone could not reach the internet and the bridge moves about 2.7 megabytes a second, so it was ten minutes of upload before anything ran. Every group of weights turned out to be exactly ternary, so the projections run on the Bonsai port’s packed kernels, unchanged. What Qwen3 needs beyond them is seven small Mojo kernels: RMS norm, the per-head q and k norm with YaRN RoPE and the KV-cache writes in one kernel, attention scores and values, SwiGLU, and the residual add. The RoPE table comes straight from MLX’s own YaRN rope, so positions match the reference exactly. On Qualcomm’s reference phone it generates 25 tokens a second, 40 milliseconds a token, reading the full 2.19 gigabytes each time. For 16 steps every greedy token matches MLX, with all 151,669 logits within 0.008, and asked why the sky is blue it gives the answer on the slide. Its prompt now runs batched on the matrix units: the ternary projections on Bonsai’s matrix kernel, and attention as two cooperative-matrix products with a softmax between. Llama’s 431-token passage, 408 tokens for Qwen3, takes 3.95 seconds instead of 17.4 one token at a time, 4.4 times faster, with the same generated tokens.
