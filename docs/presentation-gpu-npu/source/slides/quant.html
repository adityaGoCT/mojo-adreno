Why blocks lose

ORT 1.29’s QNN provider maps block int4 only as LPBQ. On 448 × 2048 × 16384: fp16 4.25 ms, int8 per channel 3.94, int4 per channel 3.75; LPBQ as MatMul 23.4 ms, as FullyConnected a crash except at 2048 × 2048.

Source: npu/README.md on branch npu-quant (7906a53) and its session notes · Llama: Galaxy S26 Ultra · Qwen3-8B: Galaxy Z Fold

Can quantized weights make the NPU faster still? Partly. With weights quantized per output channel and 16-bit activations, Llama’s prefill drops from 168 to 116 milliseconds with int8, and 111 with int4: 1.45 and 1.5 times faster than fp16, and about six times the Adreno. But accuracy suffers. The w8a16 graph’s K/V are within 3 percent of the fp16 model’s, and the text stays coherent but becomes a paraphrase after 29 tokens. Int4 per channel is too coarse for Llama. The accurate int4 form, with a scale per block of 64, only reaches QNN as low-power block quantization, and on this stack that is slow as a MatMul, 495 milliseconds, and crashes QNN as a FullyConnected for every shape but 2048 by 2048. The ternary Qwen3-8B fits the per-channel path better: its weights are already coarse, and with int4 per channel on the HTP its 408-token prompt prefills in 1.37 seconds against 3.60 on the GPU, 2.6 times faster, and all 80 generated tokens match the GPU’s exact-ternary run. These results are on the npu-quant branch, not yet merged.
