π0, the robot policy, has the same shape as a language model’s prefill plus decode. A SigLIP encoder reads three camera images, Gemma 2B runs once over the 816-row prefix of image and language tokens, and a smaller expert denoises a 50 by 32 action chunk in 10 steps against the prefix’s K/V cache. The prefix is dense matrix work over a fixed shape, so it moves to the NPU: six fp16 graphs of three layers each, compiled once. On the Galaxy Z Fold it takes 0.80 seconds instead of 2.34, 2.9 times faster, and the chunk goes from 3.6 to 2.05 to 2.16 seconds. On the X2 Elite laptop, where the Vulkan server itself runs the NPU and writes the K/V straight into the GPU’s buffers, the prefix takes 0.81 seconds against 2.13 to 2.15, and the chunk 1.82 to 1.85 seconds against 3.1 to 3.9. The actions move from 1.1e-5 to 3.2e-3 of PyTorch’s fp32 model; all of that comes from the fp16 prefix, because PyTorch’s own steps from the handed-off K/V match the device within 1.4e-6. And the GPU no longer holds Gemma 2B at all: 1.4 gigabytes instead of 5.4.
