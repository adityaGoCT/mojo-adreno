8 bits, not finer int4

Simulated, E2B’s long prompt: int4 per 32 gives a KL of 0.45 (from 0.62); int8 per 64, 0.0024.

The head stays int4

It adds only 0.006 of KL in int4, and saves 0.20 GB (E2B) or 0.34 GB (E4B) of every token’s reads.

What it costs

Decode reads 1.8× the bytes: 1.47× int4’s time (E2B), 1.53× (E4B). Prefill: 2–4% more.

Still exact

Every storage is within 1.9e-4 of transformers on its own weights, every greedy token matching.

Source: gemma4/README.md (“int8”), branch gemma4 (52c2951) · short: the sky prompt; long: 960 tokens · times from a second Fold

The gemma4 branch now has two more storages. int8 stores every matrix but the output head in 8 bits, with an fp16 scale per 64 weights like int4’s. With it both models make the bf16 models’ greedy choice at every position of the long prompt and all but one of the short one, and the mean KL divergence drops 25 to 130 times, to between 0.003 and 0.012. The storage was chosen by simulation first: a finer int4 grid barely helps, while 8 bits nearly close the gap; and every part of the model costs about the same in int4 except the head, which stays int4 to save a fifth of a gigabyte per token. Mixed also leaves the MLPs of the K/V-sharing layers in int4, and lands in between: good on the long prompt, less so on the short one. The cost is decode time: int8 reads 1.8 times the bytes and takes about 1.5 times as long, 41.1 milliseconds for E2B and 83.0 for E4B; mixed takes 33.1 and 73.0. Prefill hardly changes, 2 to 4 percent, because the matrix products, not the weight reads, set its time. These times come from one series on a second Fold, where int4 matched the first phone within a percent.
