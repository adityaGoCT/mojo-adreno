Here is everything on one table. The NPU wins wherever the work is a large dense prefill over a fixed shape: Llama’s prompt 4.1 times faster in fp16, 5.9 times with int8 weights at a cost in accuracy, the ternary Qwen3-8B’s prompt 2.6 times, and π0’s Gemma prefix 2.9 times on the phone and 2.6 on the laptop. Decode is a memory-bound loop over all the weights, and in fp16 the two processors tie, at about 63 gigabytes a second. With 4-bit weights the GPU decodes three times faster, and the NPU would need quantized graphs. And the mixture of experts goes to the GPU in both phases, because the NPU’s static graph must compute every expert; those two NPU numbers are one measured layer times 48. SmolVLA is the counter-example of a different kind: its prefix is only 62 milliseconds of a 776-millisecond chunk on the GPU, so we did not move it to the NPU at all; there is no measurement for it there.
