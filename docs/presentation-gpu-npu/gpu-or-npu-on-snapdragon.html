GPU or NPU? What we measured on Snapdragon

Mojo kernels on the Adreno 840 and X2-90 GPUs against fp16 graphs on the Hexagon NPU (HTP V81), through ONNX Runtime and QNN: a mixture of experts, Llama’s and π0’s prefill, and Gemma 4 on the phone’s GPU.

2.9×

π0’s Gemma 2B prefix on the NPU: 0.80 s against 2.34–2.35 s

16×

the work a static NPU graph does for an MoE: all 128 experts, not 8. The GPU keeps it.

a Gemma 4 E2B token in int4 on the Adreno 840; int8 closes the gap to bf16

Galaxy S26 Ultra, Galaxy Z Fold SM-F976U (Samsung Remote Test Lab) · Snapdragon X2 Elite laptop (Qualcomm Device Cloud)

Two ways onto the chip

GPU: Adreno 840, Adreno X2-90

  • Mojo DeviceContext kernels, SPIR-V from the fork’s adreno840 target, through Vulkan.
  • Decided at run time: a kernel reads the router’s expert ids; a prompt of any length.
  • int4 and int8 weights, unpacked inside the kernels.
  • Prefill on KHR cooperative matrices (fp32, 64 × 64 × 8).

NPU: Hexagon HTP V81

  • fp16 ONNX graphs through ONNX Runtime’s QNN execution provider (ORT 1.29 + QNN 2.42 from Maven Central).
  • Compiled once, on the device, for fixed shapes: 448 rows for Llama (201 s), 832 for π0 (18.5 min).
  • Compiled contexts load at each process start: 1.9–2.4 s (Llama).
  • Quantized weights only per output channel (w8a16, w4a16).

An MoE: dynamic on the GPU, static on the NPU

Qwen3-30B-A3B, int4: 48 layers, each with 128 experts; a router picks 8 per token, so a token uses 3.3B of 30.5B parameters. Snapdragon X2 Elite laptop.

3

1

All 128 experts run for every token, weighted: 16× the useful arithmetic.

And it does not fit as fp16

The whole model is 61 GB in fp16, against the laptop’s 33.6 GB. On the HTP it would need the quantized path (w4a16) and contexts of a few layers each.

The hardware is fast

The HTP streams fp16 weights at about 100 GB/s, against 55–60 GB/s for our GPU kernels, and runs 3.4 TFLOP/s of fp16 matrix work.

The graph is the limit

A decode token moves all 128 experts’ 1.2 GB a layer. Reading only 8 would be about 0.8 ms a layer (README’s estimate).

Accurate in fp16

Median token within 1.2e-3 of transformers’ fp32 layer. QK norms written as L2 norms: QNN’s RmsNorm refuses heads of 128.

Shape-dependent plans

QNN compiled the 128-token graph to a slower plan than the 256-token one: 213–218 against 95–97 ms.

So for this MoE the GPU decodes and prefills. The NPU could take the dense attention, or a prefill with expert gathering, once QNN graphs can select weights by index.

Prefill is matrix work

2,550 tokens/s on the HTP against 629 (fp16) and 672 (4-bit) on the Adreno: 4.1× and 3.8×.

Decode is memory

Each token reads 2.47 GB of fp16 weights; both run near the same memory speed, about 63 GB/s.

4-bit decode: the GPU

13.2 ms, 3× fp16. The NPU has no 4-bit row: int4 on the HTP needs quantized activations (w4a16).

fp16 on the HTP is exact enough

K/V within 2.2e-3 relative, logits within 0.027 of PyTorch: closer than ONNX Runtime’s fp16 CPU kernels.

Why blocks lose

ORT 1.29’s QNN provider maps block int4 only as LPBQ. On 448 × 2048 × 16384: fp16 4.25 ms, int8 per channel 3.94, int4 per channel 3.75; LPBQ as MatMul 23.4 ms, as FullyConnected a crash except at 2048 × 2048.

Prefix 2.9× and 2.6×

18 layers over 832 rows, 3.2 TFLOP, in 0.79 s on the HTP (4.0 TFLOP/s). The chunk: 1.7× on the phone, 1.7–2.1× on the laptop.

Accurate enough

Actions within 3.2e-3 of PyTorch fp32 (the GPU alone: 1.1e-5). All of it is the fp16 prefix: PyTorch’s steps from the handed-off K/V match within 1.4e-6.

Less on the GPU

The GPU holds no Gemma 2B weights: 1.4 GB instead of 5.4 GB. Vision and the 10 steps are now most of the chunk.

Laptop: K/V in place

The Vulkan server runs the NPU and writes K/V into the GPU’s mapped buffers: 2.4–3.3 ms beyond the graphs.

Decode reads every weight

1.21 GB in 28.1 ms (43 GB/s), 2.47 GB in 53.8 ms (46 GB/s); 425 and 509 launches a token.

Prefill on the matrix units

int4 projections on cooperative matrices are 80–86% of a prompt, at 1.7–2.1 TFLOP/s. Attention there: 0.28 s, was 1.70 s scalar.

What is new in Gemma 4

Later layers reuse earlier K/V (the last 20 / 18); per-layer inputs from a 4.7 GB table (E2B) left in the checkpoint, a row a token; sliding window 512.

The gap to bf16 is int4 rounding, not the kernels: the phone matches transformers on its own weights, and the error is spread over every kind of matrix.

Source: gemma4/README.md, branch gemma4 (52c2951, not merged) · Galaxy Z Fold SM-F976U · bf16 teacher-forced on the phone’s tokens

8 bits, not finer int4

Simulated on E2B’s long prompt: int4 per 32 only takes the KL from 0.62 to 0.45; int8 per 64 to 0.0024.

The head stays int4

It adds only 0.006 of KL in int4, and saves 0.20 GB (E2B) or 0.34 GB (E4B) of every token’s reads.

What it costs

Decode reads 1.8× the bytes: 1.47× int4’s time (E2B), 1.53× (E4B). Prefill 2–4% more: products, not reads, set it.

Still exact

Every storage is within 1.9e-4 of transformers on its own weights, every greedy token matching.

What decides GPU or NPU

Dense, fixed-shape prefill: the NPU

2.6–4.1× on Llama, ternary Qwen3-8B and π0. Overlapped with the NPU, the K/V handoff leaves 0.8–2.0 ms (Llama), 2.4–3.3 ms (π0, laptop).

Memory-bound decode: a tie in fp16

38.8 against 39.8 ms. With int4 weights the GPU is 3× faster; the NPU would need quantized graphs.

Routing, run-time shapes: the GPU

An MoE’s static graph computes all 128 experts; the GPU reads the 8 chosen and groups tokens by expert.
