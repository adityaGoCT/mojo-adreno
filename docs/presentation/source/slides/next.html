Mojo and MAX on Adreno · 29 · code: adityaGoCT/mojo-adreno · native target: adityaGoCT/modular, branch adreno-spirv-target

Where next. First, MAX on the phone by itself. The remote device proves the whole path; next, the runtime built for Android and the plugin talking to Vulkan directly, which also removes the round trip per launch. On the compiler side, the native target covers Mesa Turnip only. Qualcomm’s stock driver needs a second flavour: SPIR-V for Vulkan 1.1, and the 32-bit narrowing of index math moved into the backend, where it belongs. The same target could also emit OpenCL C, replacing our second retargeter. And the cooperative-matrix kernels, which now run on an Adreno 840, can go further: staging tiles in shared memory, fusing epilogues so more of MAX’s matmuls qualify, and integer products. For Llama, int4 weights already cut decode to about 13 milliseconds a token; fusing small kernels would cut dispatches, and the prompt, now processed as matrix products on the Adreno 840 in 0.63 seconds instead of 17.7, attention included, still has the small projections to speed up. Bonsai runs at 80 tokens a second, and its prompt is now six times faster on the matrix units; for both models, the products would go faster in fp16 if partial sums could be folded into fp32, which Qualcomm’s cooperative-matrix conversion extension would allow on drivers that offer it. Upstream, the structurizer fix we patched into LLVM belongs in LLVM itself, the strength-reduction crash deserves a small reproducer, and the warp-sum hook and the target itself are natural contributions to Modular once their compiler accepts outside changes, which they have said will be by the end of 2026. The question we started with, can Mojo reach a phone GPU, is answered. The open question is how much of MAX should follow. Thank you.
